# Hook Lab

The hook is beat 1. It runs under 10 seconds and gets 80% of the effort for the whole script. Nail it and the body writes itself. Miss it and nothing else matters, because nobody hears the body.

## Load first

- `my-context/icp.md` for the dream outcome and the pain in the buyer's words
- `my-context/winners.md` for loops and openers that already worked for this user
- `my-context/voice.md` so every line sounds like them

(Default folder: `~/.claude/my-context/`.)

## The 4 parts

A strong value hook has all four. Missing one is fine sometimes, but know which one you are missing and why.

| Part | What it does | Example piece |
|---|---|---|
| 1. High-stakes sensational angle | A stake the viewer feels. Money, clients, status, something they could lose or win. Bold, not safe. | "and it's why they don't have any leads" |
| 2. Authority, borrowed or earned | Why trust this. Earned: your result. Borrowed: a name, platform or group the viewer already trusts. | "posting on Instagram", "what the top coaches do" |
| 3. Open loop | A question the viewer needs answered, so they stay. Tease it, never give it. | "almost everyone misses the third one. So let me show you what it is." |
| 4. Specific number | Something measurable they can pin. Hooks without a number or defined metric do worse. | "$10,000 a month", "these three things" |

## Aim it at the viewer's aspiration

Put the viewer inside the hook as the person who wants the outcome.

- Weak, external: "Every creator making $10K a month is doing the same three things."
- Strong, aspirational: "If you want to make $10K a month, you need to be doing these three things."

Then check the dream outcome is the one the buyer really wants (see the want ladder in `topic-ladder`). In the example on the call, the buyer did not just want $10K a month. They wanted $10K a month **and unlimited leads**. Adding the real want made the hook land.

Note: "If you want [outcome]" is aspiration. "If you're a [job title]" is an audience callout, and it is weaker. Lead with what they want, not what they are.

## The rendition loop

1. Ask the user for their rough hook first. Their 10% comes before your 80%.
2. Score it against the 4 parts in one line each: present, weak or missing.
3. Offer two or three renditions that fix the weakest part only. Do not rewrite what already works.
4. Say each one aloud in your head. If it stumbles, fix or drop it.
5. Run the gate in `references/hook-gate.md`. Do not show a rendition that fails a BLOCK item.
6. Repeat. Three or four renditions is normal.

When the user says "I like this but [X] is missing", fix only X.

When they reject twice for a reason that is not wording (the stake is wrong, nobody cares), stop wordsmithing. The problem is the topic or the claim. Send them back to `topic-ladder`.

## How to present renditions

Keep it tight. For each option:

```
A) [the hook, as spoken]
   Parts: stake [x] / authority [x] / loop [x] / number [x]
   Weakest part: [one word]
```

Two or three options. Never ten. Then stop and wait.

## When stuck on one part

- **No loop?** Pull a loop ending from `references/hook-shapes.md` or from a past winner.
- **No number?** Count something real: steps, days, dollars, reps, clients. Never invent a result.
- **No stake?** Ask what the viewer loses if they ignore this video. Put that in.
- **No authority?** Borrow it: name the platform, the group already doing it, or the result you got.
