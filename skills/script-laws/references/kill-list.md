# Kill list

Things that have killed real hooks and scripts. Screen every draft against this before showing it. Each entry is an example of taste, not theory.

Keep this list to about 15. When the user rejects something new, add it to the user's own kill list at the end of `my-context/voice.md` in their words. Theirs outranks this one.

## Instant kills

1. **Vanity numbers as the engine.** Follower counts, downloads, "10,000 people already use this". The viewer does not care how many people did something. They care what it does for them.
2. **Academic or definition opens.** "According to research...", "The definition of X is...". Study-first and concept-first opens flop.
3. **Blanket nothing-burger statements.** "Content is changing." "Instagram hit a new level." If any account could say the line, kill it.
4. **Bolted-on payoff clauses.** A second half that feels glued on ("and you can use it to grow your business"). The payoff welds into the sentence or the sentence goes.
5. **Unknown or abstract enemy.** If there is a villain, name it at a size the viewer recognizes: the old-school gurus, the ad agencies, the algorithm change. "Some people" is not an enemy.
6. **Hollow borrowed examples.** Case studies the viewer cannot feel. Use examples from their world.
7. **Statement with no open loop.** Nothing left to wonder about means nothing to stay for.
8. **Too cute.** Clever wordplay loses to a raw, blunt claim almost every time.
9. **Receipt stuffing.** Four impressive numbers in a row. One felt number beats four.
10. **Weak verbs.** Shared, released, launched, posted, said. Use verbs with heat: wasted, missed, buried, killed, blew, stole.
11. **Choppy read-aloud.** Clipped fragments with no connective tissue. Write for the mouth: contractions in, and after the hook every beat opens with So, And, Because, But.
12. **Softened commitment.** A half-taken stance reads as fear. If you're taking the position, take it fully.
13. **Smart over blunt.** Between a clever phrasing and a raw one, raw wins. "They hid it from you" beats "it kept you distracted".
14. **Soft sneaky adverbs.** "Subtly", "under the radar", and similar filler adverbs. They read as AI writing and drain the heat. Say what happened.
15. **Safe down-the-middle takes.** A correctly worded fact is not a hook. It needs a position someone could argue with. On a heated topic, the right reaction is "I knew it", not "huh, interesting".

## Fix moves that rescue a weak draft

- **Sensationalize the verb.** "Wasted" not "spent". "Ripping you off" not "costing more".
- **Simplify to one breath.** Cut until it can be said in one go.
- **Enlarge the enemy.** A bigger, more recognizable group or system.
- **Confirm a suspicion.** What does the viewer already half-believe? Say it out loud.
- **First-person stake when it's real.** "If I was starting from zero again, this is exactly what I'd do."
- **Put it on the viewer's aspiration.** "If you want [their outcome]..."

## Soft cautions (say it, let the user decide)

- **Stale premises.** A hook whose only charge is something the viewer already fully believes ("Instagram reach is down") gives them no new reason to stay. Add a twist.
- **Repeating your last opener.** Don't open with the exact same shape as the last video you posted. Beyond that, repeating a proven shape is fine.
- **Format that only works for famous faces.** Low-effort list or rating formats work for big creators because people know them. Check the account size in the swipe file before copying.
- **Hook and body blame different things.** If the hook accuses X, the body has to prove X, not Y.
- **Frame one tells the viewer their interest is a waste.** Name their pain instead. "Your posts get views but no clients" beats "posting is a waste of time".
